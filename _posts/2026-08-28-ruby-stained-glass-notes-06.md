## Something cool happened this morning

This morning, I started assembling the first test panel in my garage.

The steps were quite straightforward:
  1. Clean the cutting oil from the glass.
  2. Stick the copper foil around each piece.
  3. Nail the pieces together on the workbench.
  4. Add solder beads between the pieces.
  5. Smooth the solder along the length of the copper foil.
  6. Turn the panel around.
  7. Repeat steps 4 through 6.
  8. Smooth the solder along the outer edge of the panel.

## What I learned today

The first thing I realized is that soldering is much harder than I remembered. I'll have to practice a bit to get the feel of the material again. 

Also, copper foil is much narrower than lead came, so I don't need to apply large beads. I ended up with too much solder, leaving large blobs around the panel. 

I'll also remember to start with the back of the panel rather than the front. It's a shame to lay nice solder lines on the front only to ruin them with the heat when you solder the other side. 

I also think I'll change two things: 
- Use a larger foil or heavier material for the perimeter. Right now, it's too thin and not very sturdy.
- Find a patina to blacken the solder so it'll match the painted lines. Right now, the solder is very bright and works against the painted black lines.

What do you think?

It's still pretty rough around the (literal) edges, but it already looks super cool.

I love how the patterns in the glass render when front-lit. I think it looks really good as a freeform panel.

The panel is a tad small because I removed the contrasting pieces around the gem. So if you — the readers of this newsletter — pick the freeform panel over the square one when I put it to the vote, I'll probably bump its size a little bit.
